[Tasting History with Max Miller](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCsaGKqPZnGp_7N80hcHySGQ) is must-watch YouTube for me. Miller is a wonderful storyteller, and his show melds interesting history lessons with period-appropriate recipes. As it turns out, he's also a fan of board games. [His new game is now live on Kickstarter](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/saltgames/max-millers-tasting-history-the-board-game/).

If you haven't watched the show before, you should give it a try. Before jumping in, you might want to know more about [hardtack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardtack) and [garum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garum). The "[clack clack](https://youtu.be/oPTdSMOQRnY?si=Ha3iN8Hl-fb2ndxa&t=1030)" sound of smacking two pieces of hardtack together comes up regularly. It's from an early episode: "[How to Eat Like a Pirate: Hardtack & Grog](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oPTdSMOQRnY)". One of the first hit episodes on the channel [described the process of making garum](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5S7Bb0Qg-oE). The ancient fish sauce was covered more recently in "[Ancient Roman Garum Revisited](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ICZww0DtQKk)", and then once more in "[The History of Fish Sauce - Garum and Beyond!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=woTh70WZ6ao)".

I highly recommend the episode "[What it was like to visit a Medieval Tavern](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iG0lgnrGHv0)". The story of how [Osbert of Elstow died by misadventure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iG0lgnrGHv0&t=938s) amuses me much more than it probably should.

All that said, the main story here is [the board game](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/saltgames/max-millers-tasting-history-the-board-game/). It's a [worker placement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_mechanics#Worker_placement) game, and Miller does a great job [describing it in the trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IbI--entMyw). It sounds fun to me! The game was [fully backed in the first five minutes](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/saltgames/max-millers-tasting-history-the-board-game/posts/4834349), so others are excited as well.

If any of this sounds interesting, you should definitely [check out the campaign on Kickstarter](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/saltgames/max-millers-tasting-history-the-board-game/description).
